## 3 Startup Ideas

### 1. MuscleShield
**One-line pitch:** Prescription muscle preservation protocol for the 30M people losing lean mass on GLP-1s.

**The specific problem:** GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy, tirzepatide) are prescribed to 30M+ Americans. Nature Reviews confirmed in 2026 that up to 40% of weight loss on these drugs is lean body mass. For older patients with pre-existing sarcopenia, this creates "sarcopenic obesity" — they hit a healthy BMI while losing the functional strength to walk upstairs. No prescriber has a good workflow for managing this. Endocrinologists are prescribing blind to muscle composition changes.

**Why now:** The Nature Reviews paper (2026) gave this clinical legitimacy. The FDA is beginning to ask prescribers about muscle monitoring protocols. GLP-1 volume keeps growing. The problem is going from "maybe concerning" to "clinical liability."

**Mechanism:** When a GLP-1 prescription is written (via EHR integration or pharmacy partner), MuscleShield triggers a muscle preservation enrollment. Patient gets: (1) a baseline body composition check via InBody scale at a partner pharmacy or at-home via phone camera + bioimpedance wearable; (2) an AI-generated progressive resistance training prescription; (3) monthly check-ins tracking creatinine, albumin, grip strength estimates. Results flow back to the prescribing physician as a monthly muscle health report. Clinicians don't have to change their workflow — they just see a flag if muscle decline is accelerating.

**First wedge:** Obesity medicine specialists and bariatric surgery centers. They already think about body composition, they're already treating GLP-1 patients, and they're most exposed to the liability if sarcopenic obesity becomes a litigation target. Reach them via the Obesity Medicine Association conference and direct outreach to the 7,000 board-certified obesity medicine physicians.

**What kills it:** Patient adherence to resistance training is terrible without in-person accountability. If patients don't do the workouts, the product becomes a monitoring tool with no intervention attached, and prescribers stop referring. Also: Eli Lilly or Novo Nordisk could build this directly as a companion app to their drugs.

**Weirder variant:** Veterinary GLP-1s are entering the market for obese dogs and cats. Same muscle loss problem, almost no competition, vet practices even less tooled-up than human medicine.

### 2. LongevityCopilot
**One-line pitch:** The vertical EHR for longevity medicine practices that standard EMRs can't handle.

**The specific problem:** Longevity medicine is going mainstream (Amazon One Medical integration being the signal), but boutique practices are operationally primitive. A typical visit covers: epigenetic biological age, telomere length, metabolomics panels, CGM data, peptide protocols, HRT titration, senolytic dosing schedules, and 3–5 wearable data streams. Epic has none of this. Clinicians are running on Epic lite + Notion + Excel + copy-paste. The implementation gap is a software problem as much as a clinical one.

**Why now:** Amazon One Medical entering the space creates urgency. When the mass-market version of longevity medicine launches, boutique practices need to either differentiate (with better clinical depth) or get absorbed. The First Longevity Clinical Trial Network just launched — creating infrastructure demand for practices to participate in research and share data. There are now ~500 longevity-focused practices in the US and the number is doubling yearly.

**Mechanism:** Vertical EHR with longevity-native data models: biological age trends across visits, biomarker reference ranges calibrated to longevity research rather than insurance codes, protocol library co-developed with leading practitioners (think: "Attia Protocol," "Bryan Johnson Blueprint" variants). AI copilot synthesizes the visit and flags deteriorating trajectories. Integrates with Function Health, Labcorp, Quest, and major wearables via FHIR. Billing module handles the cash-pay economics typical of longevity practices. Monthly subscription per provider seat.

**First wedge:** 10 founding practice partners — boutique longevity clinics spending $15K+/year on cobbled software stacks who will co-develop the product. Target: Fountain Life, Human Longevity Inc, Forward Health, RejuvenAge. They want differentiation from Amazon and they want research participation infrastructure. Start as a data aggregation layer before asking them to switch their full EHR.

**What kills it:** EHR switching is famously painful. Sales cycles stretch 12–18 months. If Epic or Athena releases a longevity module (they're watching the space), the wedge narrows fast. Also: boutique longevity practices serve affluent cash-pay patients who want a human concierge experience — they may not value software efficiency the way a high-volume clinic would.

**Weirder variant:** Flip the model — sell to patients, not practices. A "longevity passport" that patients own, carry across all their providers, and share on their terms. Let patients be the sales force that forces practices onto your platform.

### 3. PhenomeMatch
**One-line pitch:** A network of pre-qualified longevity trial candidates matched to biotech sponsors by phenotype.

**The specific problem:** Retro Biosciences, Junevity, and Altos Labs are all running or starting first-in-human longevity trials in 2026. Standard clinical trial enrollment costs $5K–$15K per enrolled patient and takes 18–24 months. Meanwhile, 10M+ people have years of longitudinal wearable/biomarker data in Oura, Apple Health, Function Health, and CGM apps — data that exactly maps to longevity trial eligibility criteria. The First Longevity Clinical Trial Network just launched and immediately faces an enrollment bottleneck.

**Why now:** The supply side (longitudinal self-tracking data) matured in 2023–2025 with FHIR becoming a real interoperability standard. The demand side (first-in-human longevity trials) is hitting its peak in 2026. Both sides are now ready.

**Mechanism:** Patients contribute their longitudinal health data via FHIR integration (Apple Health, Oura, Function Health, lab reports). Platform creates a "phenotypic fingerprint" — biological age trajectory, metabolic phenotype, organ function trends. Longevity biotech sponsors query the network with their trial inclusion/exclusion criteria and get matched, de-identified candidate pools. Patients consent to share data in exchange for: (a) cash compensation, (b) access to the interventions being trialed, (c) a "longevity trial readiness score." Sponsors pay per successful enrollment.

**First wedge:** r/longevity subscribers, Function Health members, and Levels.health alumni. These people already have years of longitudinal data, are highly motivated to participate in longevity research, and trust the concept of data-for-access trade. Reach via the longevity influencer ecosystem (Peter Attia, Bryan Johnson) with affiliate enrollment incentives.

**What kills it:** Privacy. Most people won't share longitudinal health data even for compensation once they understand the breadth of what's shared. HIPAA complexity creates legal overhead. And the real bottleneck for early longevity trials may not be enrollment — it may be IND approval and trial design, in which case solving enrollment doesn't unblock anything.

**Weirder variant:** Flip the data flow entirely. Instead of giving sponsors patient data, give patients a "trial readiness score" — personalized advice on what interventions to adopt NOW to qualify for the specific longevity trials they want to join in 18 months. Sell that as a subscription before ever touching trial operators.

## Where I Might Be Wrong

**Weakest idea: PhenomeMatch (#3).** It requires simultaneous trust from patients (share your most sensitive longitudinal health data) and confidence from sponsors (this will actually accelerate my trial). If the real trial bottleneck is FDA/IND, not enrollment, the whole premise is wrong. Verify: call two longevity trial operators and ask what's actually blocking their H2 2026 first-in-human timelines.

**MuscleShield (#1) assumes prescriber referral.** Endocrinologists are already stretched thin. Adding a step to refer patients to a muscle protocol may just not happen without a hard workflow integration or payment incentive. The real distribution question is: do you go through prescribers at all, or go direct-to-GLP-1-patient via pharmacy apps?

**LongevityCopilot (#2) has an EHR graveyard problem.** Dozens of vertical EHR companies have died waiting for practices to switch. The safer wedge might be: don't call it an EHR. Start as a data aggregation and clinical intelligence layer that sits on top of whatever EHR the practice already uses. Become indispensable before asking them to rip out Epic.

**Underlying assumption worth stress-testing:** Are longevity clinics actually growing fast enough to support a software company right now? There may be 500 practices today, but the average practice is small (2–4 physicians) and cash-constrained. The TAM might be $50M in software spend — interesting but not venture-scale unless you expand the definition.
