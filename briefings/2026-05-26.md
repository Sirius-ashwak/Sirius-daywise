## The 3 Ideas

---

### 1. Corridor
**One-line pitch:** API that makes stablecoin-to-fiat conversion invisible for mid-market B2B payments.

**The specific problem:** Mid-market companies ($20M–$500M revenue) paying cross-border suppliers are losing real money on SWIFT — 1-5 day settlement, 2-3% FX spread, compounding intermediary fees. Stablecoins solve this on paper, but the fiat off-ramp in each corridor (USD→BRL, EUR→INR, etc.) requires separate banking relationships, KYC/AML stacks, and accounting integrations. Building it in-house is a $200K+ engineering project. The large banks built private rails for themselves. Nobody built the middleware for mid-market.

**Why now:** GENIUS Act (US) + MiCA (EU) are fully in effect — legal clarity for enterprise stablecoin use is new. B2B stablecoin volume is proven and documented. IBS Intelligence specifically named the off-ramp infrastructure gap this month.

**Mechanism:** Single API — send stablecoin in, local fiat arrives in the supplier's bank account. Corridor handles exchange, local banking rails in 40+ corridors, AML/KYC on both legs, FX rate lock, and reconciliation data pushed to NetSuite/SAP/QuickBooks. Pricing: per-transaction fee + FX spread, structurally cheaper than SWIFT by 60–80%.

**First wedge:** US importers paying Asian or Latin American suppliers. Their FX pain is quantifiable and acute. Reach via freight forwarders, trade finance brokers, and AP software integrations (Tipalti, Bill.com).

**What kills it:** SWIFT launches its own stablecoin integration (actively in progress) or major banks offer this natively to mid-market clients within 18 months. The window may be 2 years wide, not 5.

**Weirder variant:** White-label for DAO treasuries that need to pay contributors in local fiat — the DAO tooling space is desperate for real-world payment rails and has no good solution.

---

### 2. Guardrail
**One-line pitch:** Drop-in compliance SDK for non-bank fintechs building on embedded finance APIs.

**The specific problem:** Every startup building on Unit, Stripe Treasury, or Synapse is responsible for their own compliance stack — transaction monitoring, SAR filing, customer due diligence updates, fraud rules. Greenboard serves 500+ banks, but the non-bank program managers (seed-stage fintechs building on BaaS) have no equivalent. They're hiring compliance consultants, building from scratch, or shipping with compliance debt. Regulators are now holding program managers — not just sponsor banks — liable. This is eating 3–6 engineers and delaying launches by 6+ months.

**Why now:** CFPB and OCC issued new program manager liability guidance in 2025. Regulators shut down several BaaS arrangements in 2024–2025, making sponsor banks demand demonstrated compliance maturity before onboarding new clients. The pressure just landed on the startups, not the banks.

**Mechanism:** Drop-in SDK that wraps your embedded finance API calls. Real-time transaction scoring using a model fine-tuned on SAR filings and FinCEN typologies. Auto-generates monthly compliance reports in the exact format your sponsor bank requires. Alerts when regulatory changes affect your specific product config. No compliance hire needed for the first 12 months.

**First wedge:** YC-backed and seed-stage fintechs building on Unit. Reach through Unit's developer community and fintech founder Slack groups. Ideal co-sell motion: Unit surfaces Guardrail to new program managers during onboarding.

**What kills it:** Unit/Stripe just builds this as a premium tier. Hard to compete with embedded distribution from the platform you're serving. Or each sponsor bank mandates their own preferred compliance vendor.

**Weirder variant:** Sell to community banks trying to launch their own BaaS programs — they need to demonstrate ongoing oversight of their program managers and have zero tooling for it. Completely different buyer, same product.

---

### 3. FloatDesk
**One-line pitch:** AI treasury agent that autonomously manages FX exposure and cross-border payments for SMBs.

**The specific problem:** Small businesses ($2M–$20M revenue) with international operations are managing FX manually — logging into multiple bank portals, getting ripped off on exchange rates (2–3% per transaction), spending 5–10 hours/week on treasury admin. Mercury and Wise Business handle simple cases, but multi-currency payables + receivables + basic hedging in one automated workflow doesn't exist for this segment. E-commerce merchants importing from Asia are losing $30K–$200K/year to FX friction they can't see or quantify.

**Why now:** Sygnum Bank's AI agent just executed live digital asset transactions on mainnet autonomously (May 20). LLM agents are reliable enough for constrained financial workflows with approval gates. Stablecoin rails provide the execution layer. GENIUS Act gives regulatory cover for USD stablecoin transactions in enterprise workflows.

**Mechanism:** Connect accounting software (QuickBooks/Xero), invoicing, and bank accounts. The agent monitors payables/receivables in real time, calculates FX exposure, and sends a weekly "treasury brief" with recommended actions and projected savings. One click = the agent executes — routing via stablecoin where cheaper, hedging with forward contracts where warranted. All execution happens through embedded finance APIs and stablecoin rails. Human approval required on every transaction over a threshold you set.

**First wedge:** Shopify merchants importing from China/Southeast Asia — clear, quantifiable FX pain, comfortable with fintech tools. Reach through the Shopify app store and import/export trade communities.

**What kills it:** Ramp or Brex adds this as a feature in their next product cycle. They have the distribution and the banking relationships. The trust problem is also real — small business owners are conservative about autonomous financial actions even with approval gates.

**Weirder variant:** Target fractional CFOs who manage treasury for 3–5 SMBs simultaneously. Give them a multi-client dashboard with AI automation. They become your distribution channel — one sale gets you 4 clients.

## Where I Might Be Wrong

Idea 1 (Corridor) is technically sound but the adoption risk is underexplored. The key unverified assumption: is the 733% YoY B2B stablecoin growth concentrated in large enterprises with dedicated crypto treasury teams, or is it actually hitting mid-market CFOs? If the former, the addressable mid-market is 18–24 months away from being ready, not today.

Idea 3 (FloatDesk) is probably the weakest. Ramp, Brex, and Mercury each have distribution advantages that are nearly impossible to overcome with a standalone product. The assumption that SMB owners will trust an AI agent with FX execution — even with approval gates — needs serious validation before building.

Idea 2 (Guardrail) has the best timing signal (explicit regulatory pressure, documented pain, no incumbent solution for program managers specifically), but the go-to-market is narrow and fragile: it requires co-sell from Unit or Stripe, which won't happen if they view it as competitive. The community bank variant might actually be the stronger angle — larger ACV, slower sales cycle, but defensible.
