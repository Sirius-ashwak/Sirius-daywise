### 1. ShelfReady

**One-line pitch:** The creator-to-retail pipeline that turns audience data into a purchase order.

**The specific problem:**
A creator with 400k engaged followers in the nutrition space wants to launch a protein pasta. They know their audience would buy it — BRAMI proved the category works. But going from "my audience wants this" to "this is on Walmart shelves" takes 2-3 years, $500k+ in ops build-out, and a lot of becoming a CPG company instead of a creator. Meanwhile, retail buyers at Target and Whole Foods are desperate for products with built-in audiences. They just don't know how to find them systematically.

**Why now:**
BRAMI's $33M raise happened *because* they had verifiable retail velocity — 400% YoY growth, provably profitable in-store. AI can now model a creator's audience (purchase intent, demographics, engagement by content type) to generate a "retail velocity prediction" before a single unit is manufactured. L'Oréal explicitly flagged creator/affiliate ecosystems as a 2026 investment theme. The demand signal is there; the infrastructure to route it to retail isn't.

**Mechanism:**
ShelfReady is a marketplace where creators with 50k-500k engaged audiences submit product concepts. An AI engine analyzes their audience data (from connected social APIs + email list + Shopify store history if they have one) to produce a retail velocity score and category placement recommendation. Retail buyers (Walmart Emerging Brands, Whole Foods Local program, Target Forward Founders) browse verified opportunities and bid for exclusive first-listing windows. A vetted co-manufacturing network handles production. Creator retains brand equity and handles content; everything else is handled. Revenue model: 3% of first-year retail GMV + SaaS fee for the buyer-side tool.

**First wedge:**
Food and beverage creators with 50k-200k followers who've publicly expressed wanting to launch a product. Reach them through food newsletter communities, YouTube cooking collabs, and the existing "creator-to-CPG" podcast circuit (Taste Radio, How I Built This adjacent).

**What kills it:**
Retail procurement cycles are brutally slow. Walmart buyers don't browse marketplaces — they take meetings from people they already know. You may end up building a sales team, not a software company.

**Weirder variant:**
Flip the direction entirely. Regional grocery chains (HEB, Publix, Wegmans) use ShelfReady to proactively scout local/regional food creators for hyper-local shelf placement — the Instagram food account with 18k local followers gets into the neighborhood HEB before going national.

### 2. CreatorFloat

**One-line pitch:** Income smoothing and real financial products built for creator revenue patterns.

**The specific problem:**
A creator making $8k/month average can't get a mortgage. Their income comes in waves — $25k in November (holiday brand deals), $2k in January (nobody's buying), nothing in March because an algorithm change tanked reach. Banks use W-2 logic. Mortgage underwriters need 24 months of "stable" income. Meanwhile, AI tools are now giving creators quantifiable recurring revenue — $4k/month from a paid AI meal-planning agent, $3k/month from a Substack, $2k/month from YouTube — but no financial product has been built to recognize this new stable layer.

**Why now:**
AI-optimized monetization is generating 35-60% earnings increases for creators adopting it — and crucially, much of that new income is *recurring* (subscriptions, paid communities, AI agents). That recurring layer finally gives underwriters something to model. Simultaneously, creator deal sizes are growing — a $40k brand deal paid net-90 is real money a creator shouldn't have to wait 3 months for.

**Mechanism:**
CreatorFloat connects to all creator income sources (Stripe, Substack, YouTube Studio, TikTok Creator Marketplace, brand deal contracts via DocuSign integration). An AI model builds a 6-month income forecast using audience health metrics, seasonality, content performance trends, and recurring revenue data. Three products built on top: (1) **Income Floor** — monthly guaranteed minimum payment, reconciled quarterly against actuals; (2) **Deal Advance** — instant payment on signed brand deals, net-90 becomes net-now at 2.5% fee; (3) **Creator Mortgage Score** — a lender-readable income certification that banks can actually underwrite. Revenue: advance fees + SaaS for the mortgage score product licensed to lenders.

**First wedge:**
Creators earning $5k-15k/month for 12+ consecutive months who've tried and failed to get a mortgage, car loan, or business loan. A single well-placed post in a creator accountant's newsletter (there are maybe 8 accountants who service 80% of mid-tier creators) seeds the first 50 users.

**What kills it:**
Underwriting is genuinely hard. One algorithm wipe, one TikTok ban, one brand scandal — and your income model is wrong. Regulatory complexity around offering financial products as a startup is slow and expensive. This likely needs a bank charter or banking partner from day one.

**Weirder variant:**
Creator income co-op. 100 creators pool into a mutual income fund. Top-earning months across the pool subsidize low-earning months for everyone. Collective bargaining leverage with brands on deal terms. Like a union, but structured as a fintech product.

### 3. LiveOS

**One-line pitch:** Shopify for creators who sell live — across every platform simultaneously.

**The specific problem:**
Live commerce in China is $550B. In the US it's $7B and has been "about to explode" for 4 years. But the infrastructure is genuinely bad: TikTok Shop live tools are clunky and TikTok-only, Amazon Live is underinvested, Instagram Live shopping was deprecated, YouTube Shopping is basic. Creators doing live selling — and there are hundreds of thousands doing it seriously — are duct-taping together 4-6 tools, manually syncing inventory, losing track of which platform got which code, and doing show recaps by hand. Nobody has built the ops layer that makes live selling as easy as posting a Shopify store.

**Why now:**
Shopify reported an 11x increase in AI-driven orders in 2026. The Cannes Creator Economy Summit is focused on creators as legitimate media businesses with real commerce infrastructure. The behavioral shift to buying during live content is undeniable — the infrastructure just hasn't caught up. Real-time AI now makes it possible to generate companion content (product cards, links, timestamped replays, follow-up email sequences) automatically during a live session, eliminating the manual post-show work that kills creator time.

**Mechanism:**
LiveOS is a creator-commerce command center. Before going live: sync product catalog across TikTok Shop, Amazon Live, YouTube Shopping, and a hosted creator storefront simultaneously. During the show: real-time inventory dashboard, coupon code generator per platform, live chat monitoring across all platforms with AI-flagged buying signals. After the show: automatic generation of show notes, product links, a shoppable replay clip, and an email sequence to non-buyers — all in one click. Revenue: $79-199/month SaaS + 0.5% of GMV over $10k/month.

**First wedge:**
Home goods, kitchen, and beauty creators who are already doing TikTok Shop lives and have a Shopify store — they're the most organized live sellers and the most frustrated by tool fragmentation. Reach through TikTok Shop seller community Facebook groups and creator manager networks.

**What kills it:**
Platform API access is the single point of failure. TikTok, Amazon, and YouTube all reserve the right to restrict third-party commerce integrations. You're building on sand that any platform can pull. TikTok Shop specifically has been aggressive about locking its ecosystem.

**Weirder variant:**
Flip the target from individual creators to brands running their own live shopping channels. A CPG brand like BRAMI does a weekly live cooking show selling direct — LiveOS becomes their backstage production OS, letting them run a QVC-style operation with 3 people instead of 30.

## Where I Might Be Wrong

**ShelfReady is probably the weakest.** The core assumption is that retail buyer behavior can be changed — that they'll browse a marketplace instead of taking warm intros. That's a hard behavioral change for a risk-averse audience. It might be more accurate to build this as a talent agency model (ShelfReady *represents* creators to retail buyers) rather than a marketplace. The marketplace framing also creates a two-sided cold-start problem. Verify by talking to 5 Whole Foods Local or Target Forward Founders buyers: do they wish they had a discovery tool, or do they already have more pitches than they can handle?

**CreatorFloat's regulatory path is longer than it looks.** Offering income floors is essentially a financial product requiring licensing in most US states. The mortgage score idea is compelling but lenders are conservative — getting even 3 lenders to accept a non-standard income certification could take 18 months. The advance product is the fastest path to revenue and probably where v1 should start entirely, letting the rest follow once you have data.

**LiveOS is the most executable** but faces the perpetual "building on rented land" risk. The question isn't whether the product is useful — it clearly is — but whether the platforms will tolerate it long enough to build a real business. Mitigation: own the creator storefront layer (the hosted creator shop) and make that the anchor, so platform integrations are addons rather than the core.
