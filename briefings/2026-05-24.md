## Three Ideas

### 1. RegentAI
**Regulatory audit infrastructure for enterprise AI agents.**

**The specific problem**: SAP just told every Fortune 1000 company to let AI agents autonomously execute their AP, HR, and supply chain processes. ServiceNow told every IT org to hand incidents to AI end-to-end. These agents are making thousands of legally consequential decisions per day — and generating zero human-readable audit trail. A HIPAA violation, SOX exception, or GDPR breach caused by an agent decision currently leaves companies with no defensible paper trail.

**Who has it acutely**: Compliance officers and General Counsels at financial services (SOX), healthcare (HIPAA), and pharma companies (FDA 21 CFR Part 11) who are actively deploying SAP RISE or ServiceNow autonomous workflows right now.

**Why now**: SAP's Autonomous Enterprise launched this month. The audit gap opens on day one of deployment. The EU AI Act requires human-overridable audit logs for high-risk AI systems, and enterprise agentic workflows that touch financial reporting or patient data squarely qualify. Enforcement is not theoretical — it's 2026.

**Mechanism**: RegentAI ships as a middleware layer (SDK + webhook hooks) between enterprise AI orchestration platforms (SAP BTP, ServiceNow Now Platform, Microsoft Power Automate) and company systems. Every agent action — decision, data access, execution — is captured, enriched with a plain-language rationale, and stored in an immutable compliance ledger. Auditors query it in natural language: "show all AP decisions over $50K in Q1 that had no human review." Compliance teams get auto-generated audit packages for SOX, HIPAA, and EU AI Act on demand.

**First wedge**: Healthcare systems running ServiceNow IT + HR autonomous workflows. Compliance-first orgs who adopted NOW Platform pre-autonomous expansion are already anxious — reach them through ServiceNow's partner network. Target: 10 design partners at $2K/month before building anything beyond an API logger.

**What kills it**: SAP and ServiceNow ship a native compliance module. This is their roadmap — "governance" is already in the ServiceNow pitch. Timeline is probably 18-24 months to native parity, which is tight but workable.

**Weirder variant**: Same infrastructure for municipal governments using AI agents to make zoning decisions, permit approvals, or benefits eligibility rulings. Public sector has even stricter transparency requirements (FOIA, public records law) and currently has zero tooling.

### 2. Vendex
**Vendor intelligence for mid-market non-SaaS procurement spend.**

**The specific problem**: A 600-person manufacturing company spends $9M/year on vendors — raw materials, contract services, logistics, facilities. Their "procurement system" is email, QuickBooks, and a shared Google Drive of PDFs. They have no idea they're 22% above market rate on MRO suppliers, that their facilities contract auto-renews in 6 weeks with a 15% price increase baked in, or that their tier-2 logistics partner is financially distressed. Coupa and SAP Ariba exist but cost $500K to implement and require a dedicated procurement team. The mid-market — 100-2000 employees, $5M-$50M in non-SaaS vendor spend — is untouched.

**Who has it acutely**: CFOs and controllers at manufacturing, construction, and professional services firms. They have real vendor risk and real spend, but no IT bandwidth and no appetite for 6-month implementation projects.

**Why now**: LLMs can now extract structured data from unstructured contracts, invoices, and vendor correspondence with commodity-cost APIs. A year ago this required expensive custom NLP. Today it's a well-designed prompt chain. The unit economics make a mid-market-priced product viable.

**Mechanism**: Vendex connects to QuickBooks, Xero, or an AP email inbox — onboarding takes one day, no IT project. It reads historical invoices, extracts vendor terms, and builds a vendor graph. It cross-references against market benchmarks and financial news feeds. Weekly, it surfaces 3-5 concrete actions: renegotiation windows, renewal alerts, supplier risk flags. The CFO reviews a plain-English list every Monday and clicks "act" or "dismiss."

**First wedge**: Manufacturing companies with 200-800 employees using QuickBooks for AP. Reach them through QuickBooks ProAdvisors — accounting firms that are trusted advisors to exactly this segment, have their clients' financial data already, and have zero competing referral programs from anyone solving this problem.

**What kills it**: Bill.com or Brex expands into vendor analytics. Both are plausible adjacencies. But neither's core motion is mid-market CFO advisory — they're payment rails companies. The cultural fit is off.

**Weirder variant**: Same system, trained specifically on government contractor procurement. GSA vendor lists are public, pricing benchmarks are public, and cost compliance is legally mandatory. Government contractors do this manually today and would pay real money for automation.

### 3. CrossOps
**Operations command center for companies scaling 100→500 people.**

**The specific problem**: When a company hits ~150 employees, someone becomes the Head of Operations (or Chief of Staff). They inherit a chaos of tools — Jira for eng, Notion for knowledge, Rippling for HR, QuickBooks for finance, Slack for everything. Their core job is to surface cross-functional operational risk and run the weekly leadership meeting. Currently, they spend 8+ hours a week pulling data from 6 tools into a Notion dashboard or Google Sheet. There's no purpose-built ops command center. Notion and Monday are too generic, Tableau requires a data team, and dedicated BI tools assume you have an analyst.

**Who has it acutely**: Heads of Operations, Chiefs of Staff, and COOs at Series B/C startups and scaling SMBs — 100-500 employees. There are roughly 40,000 of these roles in the US. They share tools aggressively with each other in professional communities.

**Why now**: Every ops person in this segment now runs 7-9 SaaS tools. The API ecosystem (Rippling, Linear, Notion, Jira, QuickBooks all have solid OAuth APIs) makes deep cross-tool integration feasible without custom engineering. The AI summarization layer that turns this data into "3 things leadership needs to discuss today" is now commodity.

**Mechanism**: CrossOps connects to the company's core stack in 2 hours with OAuth. It builds a live operational model — headcount vs. plan, hiring velocity, engineering throughput, burn rate, support ticket trends. Every Monday at 8am, it generates a 10-point "operational health" brief for the leadership meeting: anomalies flagged, trends called out, discussion questions surfaced. The Head of Ops annotates and sends. That's the core weekly loop; the rest is real-time alerting.

**First wedge**: Chiefs of Staff at Series B startups (raised $15-50M, 100-250 employees). They congregate in the Chief of Staff Network Slack, the Ops Collective, and similar communities. Offer 90-day white-glove free trials — this persona will give detailed product feedback and refer aggressively if the value is real.

**What kills it**: Linear, Notion, or Rippling ships a native "ops dashboard." Notion's "Home" concept is already moving this direction. This is the real threat — the tools being integrated against may absorb the wedge.

**Weirder variant**: Same product for nonprofit operations directors. Identical problems — fragmented tools, weekly leadership meetings, board reporting — with $0 for enterprise software. At $99/month and 8 hours saved per week, this is an easy sell. Nonprofits are an under-targeted but surprisingly dense segment.

## Where I Might Be Wrong

The weakest idea is **CrossOps**. "Ops command center" has been tried — Mosaic, Causal, and Runway for finance ops; various chiefs of staff tools — and most have struggled to hold the line between "useful dashboard" and "another thing to maintain." The assumption that the Head of Ops is a coherent enough buyer persona to build around needs verification: many of them deliberately resist single-vendor solutions because cross-tool control is their value add to the company.

The critical assumption to verify for **RegentAI** is regulatory urgency: does the EU AI Act enforcement timeline actually create immediate pressure in 2026, or is practical enforcement 2028+? If it's the latter, the "compliance deadline" hook disappears and you're left selling to CISOs who are already overwhelmed with higher-priority risks.

For **Vendex**, the key question is data access trust: will a mid-market CFO actually hand AP email inbox access to a new startup? The onboarding flow lives or dies on this. A pilot that starts with just invoice exports (not live inbox) is safer but produces lower-quality insights — worth testing both paths before committing to a distribution model.

Of the three, **RegentAI** has the sharpest buying trigger (a product launched this month that creates the problem), the clearest enterprise willingness to pay, and the most defensible position if you get the first compliance standard written around your log format.
