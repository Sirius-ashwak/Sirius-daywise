## Idea 1: Mandate

**One-line pitch:** Proof of what an AI agent was authorized to buy, captured before the chargeback fight starts.

**The problem:** Merchants who just turned on ACP/UCP checkout (Etsy, Shopify) have no way to prove what a shopping agent was actually told to do when a customer disputes an "agent bought the wrong thing" charge. Visa's chargeback threshold just tightened to 1.5%, and agent-initiated disputes count toward it — merchants can't yet tell "legitimate return" from "agent hallucinated," and have zero evidence trail to fight back.

**Why now:** ACP and UCP went live in the last year and are scaling past a million merchants. CFPB's Jan 2026 advisory made "appropriately scoped and documented" the legal bar for narrowing consumer dispute rights — but nobody has built the documentation layer merchants need to actually clear that bar.

**Mechanism:** Mandate sits as middleware in the ACP/UCP checkout flow, capturing a signed, timestamped snapshot of the agent's stated intent, constraints (price ceiling, item spec, user-approved scope), and the merchant's fulfillment match at purchase time. When a dispute lands, it auto-generates a compliance-ready evidence packet mapped to card-network agent-dispute rules.

**First wedge:** Shopify merchants who just enabled ACP and are seeing dispute volume creep — reach them via the Shopify App Store and as a co-sell with chargeback-management tools like Chargeflow or Justt.

**What kills it:** Visa, Mastercard, or OpenAI bake mandate-logging directly into ACP/UCP as a platform primitive, making a third-party evidence layer redundant.

**Weirder variant:** Point it inward at enterprise procurement agents — a tamper-proof spend log that becomes the audit trail CFOs require before letting an agent touch the corporate card at all.

## Idea 2: Solvent

**One-line pitch:** Real-time reserve attestation and AML monitoring for stablecoin issuers outside the 21-bank consortium.

**The problem:** Regional banks, neobanks, and fintechs white-labeling stablecoins via Circle or Paxos must now meet GENIUS Act/MiCA requirements for real-time reserve integrity and third-party AML oversight — without a Goldman-scale compliance team. It's acute for the dozens of smaller issuers riding the big consortium's coattails into the market without consortium-level infrastructure.

**Why now:** GENIUS Act and MiCA compliance became non-negotiable in 2026 once the big-bank consortium set the bar, and regulators are visibly enforcing — dozens of DPRK-linked stablecoin exploits have been tracked this year alone, so "we'll get to compliance later" isn't viable for anyone launching a stablecoin product this cycle.

**Mechanism:** Solvent ingests an issuer's on-chain reserve wallets and off-chain custodial statements, reconciles them continuously across chains, and publishes a live attestation feed plus AML monitoring tuned to stablecoin-specific typologies (bridge hopping, mixer exposure, sanctioned-address proximity). Issuers get a dashboard and an auditor-ready export instead of building a compliance team from scratch.

**First wedge:** Regional banks and credit unions launching white-label stablecoins through Circle's or Paxos's issuance-as-a-service programs — reach them through those platforms' partner directories.

**What kills it:** Circle or Paxos bundle this compliance layer into their own issuance-as-a-service stack for free, killing the standalone wedge.

**Weirder variant:** Point it at DAOs and on-chain treasuries instead of banks — "proof of solvency as a service" for any protocol holding a stablecoin-denominated treasury that wants to publish trustless attestations to its own token holders.

## Idea 3: Substrate

**One-line pitch:** Vertical SaaS platforms plug in industry-specific underwriting models instead of renting generic embedded-finance credit scores.

**The problem:** Vertical SaaS platforms (field service, medical billing, trucking dispatch) want to embed working-capital loans or cards, but the embedded-finance BaaS providers they plug into price risk off generic bank-transaction data that doesn't reflect what actually predicts default in that vertical — a plumber's job-completion rate or a clinic's no-show rate beats their checking-account balance as a signal.

**Why now:** Embedded finance revenue is projected to hit $51B in 2026, and "every SaaS becomes a fintech" is now the default playbook — but the underwriting layer hasn't kept pace with the volume of vertical-specific operational data these SaaS platforms already sit on, data that didn't exist in this form five years ago.

**Mechanism:** Substrate is a model marketplace and hosting layer — a vertical SaaS platform feeds in its own operational data (job records, invoices, utilization), Substrate trains and hosts a risk model specific to that vertical, and exposes a single underwriting API the platform's existing embedded-finance provider calls at loan-decision time, blended with or instead of generic bureau data.

**First wedge:** Mid-size vertical SaaS platforms already embedding a Unit/Bond/Stripe Capital-style product but complaining about approval or default rates in their own user forums — sign one vertical (field service) as a design partner, let their existing BaaS provider stay the rails.

**What kills it:** Stripe or Unit build vertical-specific model marketplaces themselves and cut out the middle layer.

**Weirder variant:** Flip it to the consumer side — a portable "vertical credit score" for gig workers that travels across platforms (a DoorDash rating feeding an Uber-embedded loan), underwriting data the worker owns instead of the platform.

## Where I might be wrong

**Solvent is the weakest of the three.** The same "what kills it" I wrote is already the market's default motion: issuance-as-a-service platforms like Circle and Paxos have strong incentive to bundle compliance tooling for free to win issuer adoption, and it's not clear there are enough mid-tier issuers willing to build their own stablecoin (versus just using a consortium-backed one) to sustain a standalone vendor. The assumption that needs verification: how many banks/fintechs outside the 21-bank consortium are actually planning to issue their *own* stablecoin in the next 12 months, versus just holding/using the consortium's.

For Mandate, the assumption to check is whether Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol or Mastercard's Agent Pay already specs out mandate-logging as part of their identity frameworks — if so, the wedge window is much narrower than it looks.
