## Idea 1 — Blast Radius
**One-line pitch:** Pre-merge simulator that tells you what an agent's PR will actually break in production.

**The specific problem:** Platform and SRE leads at 50–500 person companies. Agents now open 5–10x more PRs. Humans review the diff, but the real risk is runtime: which downstream services, cron jobs and customer configs depend on the changed behavior. Nobody sees that in a diff.

**Why now:** Agent PR volume has outrun review capacity. Production traces (OpenTelemetry) are standardized, so there's data to replay against.

**Mechanism:** Ingest traces and request logs, build a dependency and behavior map, then replay a sampled slice of real traffic against the PR in an ephemeral environment. Output is a one-screen report: "this change alters responses for 3.2% of requests, mostly the billing-export endpoint."

**First wedge:** Teams already running Datadog or Honeycomb with heavy Claude Code / Codex usage. Reach them with a GitHub App that comments on PRs. Free tier on one repo.

**What kills it:** Replay against stateful systems is hard (side effects, auth, data privacy). If setup takes more than a day, it dies.

**Weirder variant:** Sell it to insurers: price cyber/E&O premiums by a company's measured agent-change blast radius.

## Idea 2 — Gridlock Rent
**One-line pitch:** Marketplace for idle GPU cluster minutes, sold as latency-tolerant batch inference.

**The specific problem:** Neoclouds and enterprise clusters sit at 40–60% real utilization between training runs and reservations, while eval, synthetic-data and batch-agent jobs queue for capacity. Buyers can't use fragmented, short, interruptible slots; sellers can't package them.

**Why now:** This week's rounds (Crusoe, GMI, Groq) mean a lot of newly built capacity is landing before demand fully fills it. Agent workloads (long-running, retry-tolerant) tolerate preemption better than chat.

**Mechanism:** A scheduler sits in front of many clusters, splits batch jobs into checkpointed shards, and places them on gaps with a preemption-aware price. Buyers get a flat per-token or per-eval price with a deadline, not a GPU-hour.

**First wedge:** Eval and synthetic-data teams at mid-size labs and startups. Approach neocloud ops leads directly with "monetize your gaps, zero change to your reservations."

**What kills it:** Neoclouds won't want to expose utilization data or undercut their own list price. Margins are thin if the hyperscalers launch a spot tier for the same.

**Weirder variant:** Same scheduler for robot fleets' onboard compute: rent parked warehouse robots' GPUs overnight.

## Idea 3 — Teleop Tape
**One-line pitch:** Turn warehouse robots' human-takeover moments into a licensed training-data stream.

**The specific problem:** Deployers of semi-humanoids in logistics (the Avatar Robotics kind of buyer) have operators step in whenever the robot fails. Those interventions are the most valuable data in robotics, and today they are thrown away or locked inside the vendor's stack.

**Why now:** Robotics funding is at record levels and every foundation-model team is data-starved on failure cases. Deployed fleets are big enough to matter.

**Mechanism:** An on-robot logger captures sensor streams and the operator's corrective actions at each takeover, auto-labels them by failure type, strips faces and other PII, and sells the pooled, deduped sets to model builders. Warehouse customer gets a rev share plus a failure report.

**First wedge:** 3PL warehouses running pilot fleets from small robot vendors who lack a data team. Reach through the vendors: "we ship the logger, you get a cut."

**What kills it:** Robot vendors consider the data their moat and refuse. Contracts around customer-site footage are brutal.

**Weirder variant:** Skip robots: license the same takeover data from human drivers overriding ADAS in fleet trucks.
