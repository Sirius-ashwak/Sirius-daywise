## The 3 Ideas

---

### 1. LLMIndex
**Real-time visibility dashboard for where your D2C brand appears (or disappears) in AI shopping recommendations.**

**The specific problem:** 37% of consumers now start product searches in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, or Gemini — not Google. Structured product data increases AI citation rates by 3.2x, but most D2C brands have no idea whether they're being recommended or ignored. SEO tools (Ahrefs, Semrush) track Google rankings in near-real-time. Nothing equivalent exists for AI recommendations, which change with every model retraining cycle.

**Who has it acutely:** D2C brands spending $50K+/month on paid social who've noticed traffic from social flattening but can't diagnose why organic discovery is broken. CMOs at supplement, wellness, apparel brands — categories where AI assistants are already answering "what's the best X for Y."

**Why now:** The ACP (OpenAI + Stripe) and UCP (Google + Shopify) just launched in early 2026. Shopify made "Agentic Storefronts" the default for all merchants. The infrastructure for AI-native commerce just landed — but the analytics layer doesn't exist yet. This is the same window SEO analytics startups had in 2005-2008.

**Mechanism:** LLMIndex queries GPT-4o, Claude 3.7, Gemini 2.0, and Perplexity daily with 500+ category-specific prompts ("best magnesium for sleep under $40," "affordable SPF moisturizer for oily skin"). It tracks which brands get cited, with what language, in what context. Brands see a citation score, trend lines, competitor benchmarks, and a structured action list (which pages to rewrite, which reviews to syndicate, which comparison content to create). SaaS at $299-$2,999/month depending on brand size and prompt volume.

**First wedge:** Supplement and wellness D2C brands ($5M-$50M revenue) already running sophisticated SEO who are analytically motivated. Reach them through the communities they're already in: DTC newsletter (Turner Novak), Operator newsletter, My First Million listeners. One case study showing AI citation lift → trial.

**What kills it:** OpenAI, Google, or Shopify launches their own "brand analytics for AI" product. Google's SGE effectively killed a generation of featured-snippet optimization agencies. Same risk here — you're building on top of systems that could cut you out.

**Weirder variant:** Flip it into offense. Don't sell monitoring — sell the content that gets you cited. Become an "AI citation agency" that writes the specific comparison articles, structured FAQs, and review content that LLMs pull from. Service business, not SaaS, but higher margins and stickier.

### 2. ProofLayer
**A behavioral authentication system that tells brands whether a creator's content is actually human-made before the wire transfer goes out.**

**The specific problem:** Brands are paying $50K-$500K per creator partnership. AI slop is rampant and getting harder to detect — not because the content is bad, but because creators are outsourcing production to AI while maintaining the appearance of authenticity. Existing AI detection tools (Originality.ai, etc.) are gameable in minutes. Brand safety teams need something that isn't just text classification.

**Who has it acutely:** Influencer marketing agencies managing brand budgets. The person who has to explain to a client why their $200K campaign featured AI-generated content with 40% bot engagement. Also, brand managers at CPG and DTC companies with seven-figure creator budgets and zero verification process.

**Why now:** YouTube removed slop channels at scale in early 2026. The FTC is tightening disclosure rules for AI-assisted content. Insurance products for creator campaigns are starting to emerge (campaign failure coverage) — and insurers need underwriting data. The market is creating demand for verification infrastructure.

**Mechanism:** ProofLayer connects to a creator's accounts via API and analyzes behavioral fingerprints across 90 days: posting time variance, language drift (does vocabulary shift in ways consistent with different writers?), caption-to-engagement timing patterns, historical voice consistency scores, and cross-platform coherence. It doesn't classify individual pieces of content as AI or human — it assigns a "Creator Authenticity Score" (0-100) with a detailed breakdown. Brands run it before signing any deal above $10K. $199 per verification, or $1,500/month for agency plans with unlimited verifications.

**First wedge:** Mid-size influencer marketing agencies (5-50 employees) who manage $1M-$20M in annual creator spend and are terrified of a slop scandal. Reach them at Influencer Marketing World, through the ANA (Association of National Advertisers), and through the "agency Slack" communities that already exist for brand safety.

**What kills it:** The arms race problem. As soon as ProofLayer's signals are known, creators (or their agencies) will optimize against them. The score becomes meaningless in 18-24 months unless the signal set keeps evolving. This is the CAPTCHA problem: verification and evasion co-evolve.

**Weirder variant:** Turn it around — sell to creators, not brands. Creators who score high get a "Human Verified" badge they can display to command higher rates. Creators pay to prove they're human. Like an SSL certificate but for authentic content.

### 3. Swarm
**A coordinated network of micro-creators that makes D2C brands appear in AI shopping recommendations — through real, disclosed, authentic content at scale.**

**The specific problem:** Two things are simultaneously true: (1) micro-creators (10K-200K followers) have higher engagement and more trusted recommendations than mega-influencers, but they earn below living wage and can't get brand deals; (2) D2C brands need "comparison content" and "review breadth" to get cited by AI shopping assistants, and individual creator posts don't generate enough citation density. The infrastructure connecting these two isn't built.

**Who has it acutely:** The supplement brand spending $15K/month on influencer campaigns seeing diminishing Instagram returns while watching Perplexity tell their customers about their competitor. And the 500K-follower fitness creator who wrote three honest reviews of protein powders, got 0 brand interest, and is considering quitting.

**Why now:** LLMs cite "comparison content" more than any other content type when recommending products. AI crawlers are indexing creator content. The ACP/UCP protocols mean AI agents now need structured, discoverable, third-party content to make purchase recommendations. This content type is undersupplied and you can build the factory.

**Mechanism:** Swarm operates a curated network of vetted micro-creators organized by niche (fitness, skincare, pet care, home goods, etc.). Brands commission "citation campaigns" — not vanity posts, but a structured package of reviews, comparisons, tutorials, and FAQ content distributed across 50-150 creators simultaneously. All content is FTC-disclosed. Swarm tracks AI citation lift (using LLMIndex-style monitoring) as the primary KPI, not impressions or likes. Creators earn $200-$2,000 per assignment based on their engagement track record. Brand campaigns start at $25K. Swarm takes 35%.

**First wedge:** Pet care and supplement D2C brands — categories where AI shopping assistants are already the primary discovery channel and where comparison content is the content type consumers trust most. Reach brand CMOs through Operators/DTC newsletters; reach creators through Discord communities and subreddits like r/influencermarketing.

**What kills it:** FTC disclosure rules make coordinated multi-creator campaigns legally complex even when disclosed. If the FTC rules that simultaneous brand-paid reviews constitute deceptive coordination (regardless of disclosure), the whole model is at risk. Also: creator content quality variance is a nightmare to QC at scale.

**Weirder variant:** White-label the network as infrastructure for PR agencies. Agencies already run "seeding campaigns" — Swarm becomes the AI-era version, productized and citation-optimized. Agencies have the brand relationships; you supply the creator network and the measurement.

## Where I Might Be Wrong

**ProofLayer is the weakest idea.** The core assumption — that behavioral signals can reliably distinguish AI-assisted from human-created content — needs serious verification. Every AI detection company has struggled with this arms race, and the false positive rate is a liability: wrongly flagging a legitimate creator destroys trust fast. Also, the market might not be willing to pay for verification if they can't enforce it — a brand gets a low score and... what? They already signed the contract.

**LLMIndex assumes LLM query patterns are stable enough to build a product around.** If OpenAI changes how GPT surfaces product recommendations quarterly, the monitoring data becomes noisy and the benchmarks meaningless. The whole value proposition rests on the recommendations being consistent enough to track over time — which isn't guaranteed.

**Swarm's unit economics need modeling before conviction.** At $25K/campaign, 35% margin, and 50-150 creators, you're paying out $16-25K in creator fees plus platform costs. That's thin. The business only works if campaigns are repeatable (brands run 4-6x/year) or if the average campaign size scales to $75K+. That needs enterprise brands, not mid-market D2C.

Of the three, **LLMIndex is the one I'd bet on first** — it's measurable, there's an established playbook (SEO SaaS), and the market timing is almost too obvious. The risk is that obvious markets attract obvious competition fast.
