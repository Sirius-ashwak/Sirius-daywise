## Idea 1: Mandate Cover

**Pitch:** Refund insurance for merchants who accept agent-placed orders, priced on the quality of the buyer's signed mandate.

**Problem:** Merchants turning on agentic checkout (Spreedly, Shopify-style channels) eat disputes with no evidence trail. "My agent bought the wrong thing" has no chargeback reason code. Small DTC brands with 20–40% margins feel this most.

**Why now:** Agent-order channels went live at PSPs this quarter, and Baselayer-style identity tooling is being funded. Card networks haven't shipped dispute rules for delegated agents.

**Mechanism:** The merchant plugin reads the agent's signed mandate (scope, cap, expiry, user confirmation) at checkout and scores it. Good mandates get auto-accepted and covered. Weak ones get step-up or decline. We take a premium on GMV, pay out refunds, and use the loss data to reprice.

**First wedge:** ~50 Shopify brands already getting agent traffic (see referrer logs). Reach them via Shopify app store plus DTC operator communities. Messy v1: manual claims, one underwriting rule.

**What kills it:** Networks or PSPs define a standard liability shift and give it away free. Or agent-order volume stays tiny for another year.

**Weirder variant:** Reverse it: sell agents (or their owners) a "bond" so agents with good history get merchant-side priority and discounts. A credit score for software.

## Idea 2: Second Bank

**Pitch:** Hot-standby sponsor bank for stablecoin and virtual-account fintechs, so one bank exit doesn't freeze customers.

**Problem:** Small rails companies, like Diameter with 20 people and 2–3 sponsor banks, depend on banks that can de-risk overnight. Their end users (Argentina, Nigeria, Poland) have money stranded when that happens. Synapse is the memory everyone has.

**Why now:** GENIUS rulemaking is pushing banks to re-paper stablecoin relationships before Jan 2027. Volume is flowing through thin sponsor-bank layers. Banks are more willing to take fee-share programs than in 2024.

**Mechanism:** A ledger-and-KYC abstraction sits above two or more sponsor banks. Account numbers are alias-routed. Customer data and reconciliation are mirrored, and pre-approved migration playbooks are done with each bank. If bank A pauses, accounts re-home in days, not months. Charge a retainer plus basis points on volume.

**First wedge:** Stablecoin on/off-ramp startups with $1B+ annualized volume and one sponsor bank. Reach them through crypto payments conferences and their investors (CMT, Lightspeed Faction portfolios).

**What kills it:** Banks refuse to be the "standby" with no revenue. Or fintechs won't pay for insurance until they've been burned.

**Weirder variant:** Sell the same failover to the banks: a "program acquirer" so a bank can dump a risky program to a pre-vetted successor bank in 72 hours.

## Idea 3: Reserve Desk

**Pitch:** Compliance and reserve-reporting stack for small GENIUS-regulated stablecoin issuers, live before January.

**Problem:** Credit unions, regional banks, and fintechs planning branded payment stablecoins face reserve attestations, redemption policy, and a FinCEN/OFAC AML program. The compliance team for this doesn't exist yet at most of them.

**Why now:** Final OCC rule is targeted for November, effective date no later than 2027-01-18, and AML rules are in proposal form now. There's a real 90-day scramble to file applications and stand up controls.

**Mechanism:** Read-only connectors to custodians and bank accounts produce daily reserve-vs-liabilities reports, a redemption SLA monitor, and sanctions-screening logs mapped to the proposed FinCEN/OFAC program. Output is examiner-ready packets. Built to the proposed rules, then patched when finalized.

**First wedge:** State-regime candidates (under the $10B threshold) and credit union service orgs. Reach them through CUSO networks and state regulator comment periods.

**What kills it:** Bridge, Paxos and other white-label issuers bundle compliance, so nobody needs their own. Or final rules move the target and the product is obsolete.

**Weirder variant:** Point it at corporate treasuries. Any company holding stablecoins as cash gets a daily "is my issuer actually solvent" monitor.

## Where I might be wrong

Idea 3 is the weakest: it lives or dies on white-label issuers not bundling compliance, and I haven't verified how many small institutions plan to issue directly rather than white-label. Idea 1 hinges on agent-order volume being real for small merchants. Circle's $0.31 average payment suggests most agent traffic is machine micro-payments, not consumer purchases, so the dispute pain may be premature. Verify first: pull agent-referred order share from 10 Shopify merchants. Idea 2's assumption is that sponsor-bank fragility is a felt pain, not a theoretical one.
