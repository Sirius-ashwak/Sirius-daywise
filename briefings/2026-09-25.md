## Three ideas

### 1. Backpressure
**Pitch:** CI that triages and gates the flood of AI-agent PRs before it drowns your reviewers.

**The problem:** Small, agent-heavy engineering teams (3–15 people running Claude Code / Codex / Cursor agents in parallel) now generate more PR volume from agents than from humans. They don't have Linear's platform team to rebuild CI around it, so PRs pile up unreviewed or get rubber-stamped.

**Why now:** Agentic coding got cheap and fast enough in 2026 (see Fable 5.1's 45% agentic cost cut this month) that a handful of engineers can run a dozen concurrent agent sessions. That volume didn't exist 18 months ago — this is a brand-new failure mode, not a scaled-up old one.

**Mechanism:** Sits in the CI pipeline as a risk classifier: semantic-diffs each PR against the actual blast radius (touches auth/payments vs. touches only test fixtures), runs only the tests statistically likely to catch a regression on that diff, and auto-merges low-risk agent PRs while routing high-risk ones to a human with a synthesized "what changed and why" summary instead of a raw diff.

**First wedge:** Seed-stage, agent-native startups already drowning in their own PR queue — reach them through the X/HN threads where people are already complaining about this by name.

**What kills it:** One bad auto-merge that breaks prod kills trust immediately — teams will switch auto-merge off forever and you're left as "yet another CI dashboard."

**Weirder variant:** Same triage engine, pointed at AI-drafted legal contracts and compliance documents at law firms — identical shape (huge draft volume, few qualified reviewers, need risk-tiered routing), completely different domain.

---

### 2. SimDiff
**Pitch:** A regression-testing harness that replays real robot failures in simulation before every fleet OTA push.

**The problem:** Robotics companies riding this year's funding wave (Skild AI, Neura, Saronic, AIDIN, D-Robotics) are now shipping VLA (vision-language-action) model updates to physical fleets on software cadence — sometimes weekly. A bad update doesn't just ship a bug, it can damage $50K+ hardware or hurt someone. Today, validation is mostly a human watching failure video clips.

**Why now:** 2026 is the year robot *behavior* became a software artifact (a model weights update) instead of a hardware change. That's exactly the shift that caused the CI crisis on the coding-agent side — except robotics has no equivalent of a test suite yet, because the "tests" are physical-world edge cases nobody has systematically captured.

**Mechanism:** Continuously ingests onboard sensor/video logs from the fleet, auto-clusters near-misses and failures into a growing regression suite, replays candidate policy updates against that suite in simulation (Isaac Sim / Genesis-class tooling), and gates the OTA push on pass rate against known failure modes.

**First wedge:** Mid-size warehouse/logistics robot fleets — not humanoid moonshots — who already have hundreds of failure clips sitting unreviewed in S3 buckets. Reach through robotics-ops Slack/Discord communities and ROS forums, not VC intros.

**What kills it:** Fleet hardware and sensor stacks are wildly heterogeneous machine to machine; a sim-replay harness that actually generalizes across rigs could turn into bespoke integration services wearing a SaaS costume.

**Weirder variant:** Apply the same "mine real failures into a regression suite" idea to LLM coding agents themselves — auto-capture production agent failure traces and replay them as an eval gate before upgrading the underlying model.

---

### 3. Meter
**Pitch:** A real-time spend-and-behavior governor sitting between your org and every AI coding agent session.

**The problem:** Engineering orgs running many concurrent agent sessions have no aggregate visibility, no way to kill a runaway loop mid-session, and no per-team budget enforcement. Cost optimization is the clearest unresolved pain point on HN right now, but the current fixes are single-developer wrapper scripts, not org-level infrastructure.

**Why now:** Model costs are dropping fast (Fable 5.1's cache-read cost down 75% this month) which is *increasing* usage volume faster than anyone is tracking it — finance teams are starting to ask why the model API bill is 4x last quarter and nobody can answer per-team.

**Mechanism:** A transparent proxy in front of agent API calls that tags every request with team/project/task metadata, applies policy (auto-downgrade model tier past a budget threshold, kill sessions a cheap "is this converging" classifier flags as stuck in an unproductive loop), and rolls it into a dashboard finance can actually read.

**First wedge:** 50–200 person eng orgs that just got a shock model-API invoice and are one Slack message from banning agentic tools outright. Reach with a "we cut agent spend 40%" case study aimed at platform/eng-ops teams.

**What kills it:** Anthropic/OpenAI build this natively into their enterprise consoles and give it away free — the most likely and most immediate failure mode, since they have every incentive to own this layer.

**Weirder variant:** Point the same governor at physical robot fleets — throttle or kill individual robots mid-task when the same "is this converging" classifier flags a non-convergent or battery/compute-wasteful action loop.

---

## Where I might be wrong

**Meter is the weakest of the three** — it's the one most exposed to a platform risk that isn't hypothetical: Anthropic and OpenAI already ship usage dashboards, and org-level spend controls are a natural, low-effort extension for them to bundle for free. The assumption that needs verification before building this is whether the "is this converging" loop-detection classifier is defensible IP or just a feature checkbox a vendor adds in one sprint. Backpressure's risk is more about trust (one bad auto-merge) than platform risk, and SimDiff's is more about integration cost than demand — both feel like they'd survive a vendor shrug in a way Meter might not.
