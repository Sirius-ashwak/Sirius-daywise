## Three ideas

### 1. Runway
**One-line pitch:** At-home ovarian-aging biomarkers plus an AI-built roadmap, for women who are done guessing about their fertility window.

**The problem:** Women 35–45 get a 10-minute OB-GYN visit and a symptom-tracking app (Midi, Alloy) — neither of which tells them *where they are* on their own reproductive aging curve or what actually moves the needle. The biology (AMH, ovarian reserve trajectories) is understood; nobody's packaged it into something a person can act on continuously.

**Why now:** AI-native biotech platforms (Isomorphic's $2.1B round, Earendil's $787M) have made biomarker-interpretation modeling cheap enough to run on a niche indication instead of only blockbuster oncology targets. Meanwhile Midi Health proved women will pay $100M-round money for access — but access without biology is a ceiling, not a moat.

**Mechanism:** Quarterly at-home blood draw for AMH/FSH/estradiol trio, combined with wearable cycle and sleep data (Oura/Apple Watch Ultra 4), fed into a model trained on longitudinal reproductive-aging cohorts to generate a personalized "runway" — not just a number, but what specifically (sleep, stress, supplementation, fertility preservation timing) shifts it.

**First wedge:** Women already paying out-of-pocket for Modern Fertility-style panels but frustrated the report is a PDF, not a plan. Reach through fertility-focused creators on TikTok/Substack and a data-partnership pitch to Midi/Alloy (they own distribution, you own the biology layer they don't have).

**What kills it:** The moment you say anything predictive about fertility, you're arguably a diagnostic device — FDA scrutiny, plus the emotional and legal stakes of being wrong are enormous. OB-GYNs may see you as competition rather than a referral partner.

**Weirder variant:** Same reproductive-aging-clock approach, aimed at high-value breeding livestock and racehorses — a market that already pays five figures for genetic testing and has zero regulatory friction about "predicting" reproductive decline.

### 2. Panel
**One-line pitch:** The operating system independent longevity clinics don't have — protocols, panels, and billing in one place.

**The problem:** Hundreds of independent longevity/healthspan clinics have opened in the wake of Neko Health and Fountain Life, but each one is stitching together generic EHR software, a spreadsheet for protocol tracking, and manual blood-panel interpretation. The deal data says the *demand* is real (31% of longevity deal count) but the *economics* aren't (6% of capital) — because nobody's fixed the ops layer.

**Why now:** LLMs are now good enough to turn a 40-marker blood panel plus wearable data into a draft clinician-reviewed protocol in minutes, not hours — the same capability class that's powering AI drug discovery at the frontier is cheap enough to run as a clinic back-office tool.

**Mechanism:** Clinics upload lab results and wearable exports; Panel auto-drafts a plain-language protocol summary and trend report for the clinician to review and send, alongside membership billing and visit scheduling built around longevity-specific workflows (quarterly panels, supplement/peptide tracking) that generic EHRs don't model.

**First wedge:** 3–5 independent (non-mega-funded) clinics as design partners, sourced through longevity clinic communities and conferences like the Health Optimization Summit — free software for case studies and a testimonial.

**What kills it:** Selling into small independent clinics is a slow, fragmented SMB motion, and if the well-funded chains (Neko, Fountain Life) build this in-house instead of buying, your addressable market shrinks to exactly the clinics that can least afford software.

**Weirder variant:** Repackage the same protocol-drafting engine as a B2B executive-health product for Fortune 500 leadership physicals — same workflow, corporate budget instead of a clinic membership fee.

---

### 3. Control Group
**One-line pitch:** Turn your own biohacking into an actual experiment — continuous non-invasive sensors plus AI-designed n-of-1 trials.

**The problem:** Serious self-experimenters (peptides, rapamycin, supplement stacks) are tracking obsessively but have no rigor — no baseline period, no washout, no way to tell signal from noise. It's why "biohacking is overhyped" is a recurring gripe: people spend money on protocols they can't actually prove are doing anything for *them specifically*.

**Why now:** Non-invasive continuous biosensing just crossed a real threshold — breath-based glucose (PreEvnt's isaac), OTC CGMs cleared by FDA, and next-gen implantables in trials — so you can finally get lab-grade longitudinal data without needles. Combine that with AI that can now design and adapt a proper single-subject trial (baseline, intervention, washout, crossover) in real time.

**Mechanism:** User stacks 2-3 non-invasive continuous sensors (glucose, HRV/sleep via wearable, a home blood-spot panel); the platform designs an n-of-1 protocol around whatever they're already taking, tells them when to hold a baseline vs. intervene, and reports back with actual statistical confidence instead of a vibe.

**First wedge:** The hardcore quantified-self/biohacker community (r/Biohackers, peptide and rapamycin self-experimenters) who already track everything but are primed by the "is this a waste of money" backlash — position as "prove it or stop paying for it," distribute through biohacking podcasts and those same skeptical Reddit threads.

**What kills it:** Single-subject inference from noisy consumer biosensor data is genuinely hard — there's a real risk the product just dresses up correlation as rigor, and once one prominent skeptic calls that out publicly, the whole pitch (rigor) becomes the liability.

**Weirder variant:** Sell the identical trial-design + sensor infra to pharma sponsors running decentralized Phase IV / real-world-evidence studies — same n-of-1 statistical engine, institutional budget instead of a biohacker's credit card.

## Where I might be wrong

**Runway** is the weakest of the three. The core unverified assumption: that women 35–45 will pay meaningfully out-of-pocket for ovarian-aging biomarkers *plus* an AI-generated roadmap, when FemTech overall still can't crack more than 3% of digital-health spend and insurance won't touch anything framed as predictive. Midi's success is an access-and-empathy story, not proof that people will pay for a biological forecast — that's the part to verify before building anything, probably via paid pre-orders or a landing-page test before writing a line of the AI-interpretation layer.
