## Idea 1 — Undone

**One-line pitch:** Escrow and recourse layer for AI-agent stablecoin payments — chargebacks for machines.

**The problem:** Companies running spend-capable agents against paid APIs (data feeds, inference, scraping) have no recourse when a machine-to-machine x402 payment goes bad — bad data, no delivery, double-billing. On-chain stablecoin settlement is instant and irreversible; card dispute rules don't apply. Finance/eng leads shipping autonomous agents are the ones exposed.

**Why now:** x402 hit 150M transactions in 9 months with zero dispute infrastructure built on top. The rails matured faster than the trust layer.

**Mechanism:** A proxy sits between the agent wallet and the merchant API. It holds payment in short-window escrow (seconds, not days), waits for a delivery receipt/webhook confirming the service was actually rendered, then releases funds — or auto-refunds on timeout/mismatch. API providers stake collateral into a shared pool that gets slashed on confirmed bad service, building a reputation score other agents can check before paying.

**First wedge:** Dev teams running spend-capable agents against paid data/inference APIs. Reach them by shipping as a drop-in proxy/middleware for the x402 ecosystem — go where the protocol's early adopters already are.

**What kills it:** x402 volume stays a niche protocol, or Coinbase/Visa/Cloudflare bundle recourse natively into their spend-limit products and give it away for free, since they own the rails already.

**Weirder variant:** Same escrow + reputation mechanic, but for agent-to-agent labor instead of API calls — an "Upwork for subagents" where one AI hires another for a subtask with automatic escrow and slashing on bad work.

## Idea 2 — Custodian

**One-line pitch:** Wallet provisioning and spend-policy engine so CFOs can let AI agents pay for things without losing sleep.

**The problem:** Enterprises want agents that autonomously pay for compute, data, or SaaS seats, but wallet setup is manual, and there's no compliance-grade way to say "this agent can spend up to $50/day on category X, above that a human signs off" — let alone trace every transaction back to a cost center after the fact. Ops/finance leaders who've already been burned by a runaway agent API bill feel this acutely.

**Why now:** Stablecoin rails are mature (Stripe/Bridge, Circle, BVNK) and every major player is separately building spend-limit primitives, but nobody's shipped the policy + audit layer that makes a compliance team comfortable signing off on autonomous spend.

**Mechanism:** An SDK that provisions a wallet per agent instance in seconds, attaches programmable policy (rate limits, category allowlists, human-approval thresholds), and streams every transaction into an audit trail mapped to a human owner and cost center — the thing an internal auditor or examiner would actually ask for.

**First wedge:** Mid-market companies running fleets of internal agents for procurement/ops — integrate as an "add a wallet to your agent" plugin for agent orchestration frameworks, where the pain is already being felt.

**What kills it:** Coinbase or Stripe bundles this as a free feature of their own agent-commerce stack, or enterprises decide the safer move is banning autonomous agent spend outright rather than governing it.

**Weirder variant:** Consumer version — a family agent wallet where parents set spend policy for their kids' personal shopping/subscription agents, same policy engine, totally different buyer.

## Idea 3 — Filed

**One-line pitch:** Compliance-as-infrastructure for embedded finance vendors selling into community banks and credit unions.

**The problem:** Community banks and credit unions want AI-personalized embedded investing/lending fast, but every new feature re-triggers a full compliance review from scratch at institutions with tiny compliance teams. The bottleneck isn't tech, it's paperwork — and it's killing deal velocity for the embedded finance vendors trying to sell in.

**Why now:** Embedded finance BaaS platforms are proliferating this quarter (FIS Q4 launch, a credit-union group's new BaaS platform this week), and AI compliance copilots are now good enough to draft live regulatory mappings — but nobody's aimed one specifically at the embedded-finance-into-small-institution sales motion.

**Mechanism:** A layer embedded finance vendors plug in that auto-generates regulatory documentation for each new feature (Reg E, BSA/AML, state lending mapping), and continuously monitors production behavior against the filed compliance model — flagging drift before an examiner does.

**First wedge:** Sell to the embedded-finance vendors themselves (like the InvestiFi-style investing/lending module makers), not the banks — it shortens their own sales cycle into credit unions, so it's a growth tool for them, not just an expense.

**What kills it:** Large BaaS platforms (FIS, Marqeta, Unit) bundle compliance tooling natively and cut off the wedge, or examiners simply don't trust AI-generated compliance documentation yet.

**Weirder variant:** Point it inward — sell the same compliance copilot directly to bank innovation labs for auditing their own AI lending/investing agents' decisions, not just the vendor integrations.

## Where I might be wrong

**Filed** is the weakest of the three. Compliance software is a notoriously slow, relationship-driven sale, and the wedge here — selling to embedded-finance vendors instead of banks — assumes those vendors want to outsource compliance tooling rather than treat it as core IP and defensibility. That might be backwards: compliance moat is explicitly called out this year as what investors are funding *for*, which cuts against vendors wanting to hand it to a third party. Worth a few cold conversations with an embedded-finance vendor's compliance lead before building anything. **Undone** rests on an assumption that needs checking too: that x402 volume and irreversible-payment disputes are frequent enough in practice, today, to justify an escrow layer — if most current x402 traffic is low-stakes and low-value (as PYMNTS suggests, "not from AI agents" yet), the pain might be 12-18 months away, not now.
